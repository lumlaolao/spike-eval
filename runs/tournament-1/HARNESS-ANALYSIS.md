# Tournament 1 — Harness & Model-Infra Forensic Analysis

Scope: 7 tournament matches (QF-1..4, SF-1, SF-2, F-1), 8 models, 575 logged decisions, cross-checked against `.jsonl` eval records and Forge's ground-truth `.log` files. Harness under review: `PlayerControllerLLM` in `forge-overlay/forge-gui-desktop/src/main/java/forge/spike/LlmSpikeGui.java` (2208 lines). All findings below were independently re-verified against source lines, jsonl fields, and log excerpts before inclusion; two clusters submitted for review were refuted and are reported honestly in §7.

## 1. Executive summary

The tournament's outcomes are largely trustworthy — no match was decided purely by an infra failure — but the harness has real, fixable defects that distort fairness across models and hide the game's most consequential decisions from the model entirely. The single biggest problem is not model quality, it is uniform configuration: one `max_tokens=2048`, one 90s deadline, one retry, applied identically to terse and verbose reasoners alike, which cleanly splits the field into models that never fail (opus, gemini, qwen, claude-fable) and models that fail constantly (deepseek, kimi, gpt-5) — confirmed by a same-match, same-bridge natural experiment (QF-2: deepseek 10 non-answers vs. qwen 0). The second biggest problem is that several of the game's highest-leverage decisions — Annihilator-6 sacrifices, combat-damage assignment — are hardcoded to bypass the model entirely and go to Forge's built-in AI, in direct tension with the project's own "never fall back to AI for strategy" rule. Observability is also unreliable: double-failures on reactive (opponent's-turn) windows can vanish from both the `.log` and the `.jsonl` with no trace at all, and reported latency figures conflate real think-time with dead-retry time.

Ranked by severity/frequency:

1. **D1 — Uniform OpenRouter config (2048 tok / 90s / 1 retry) causes model-specific bridge instability** (76 events across 5 matches; fairness-distorting, not cosmetic).
2. **C1 — Annihilator/Edict sacrifice and combat-damage assignment hardcoded to Forge AI, never routed to the model** (7 instances, 4 matches; the single clearest "never fall back to AI" violation).
3. **C5 — Silent decision drops: double-failures on reactive windows leave zero jsonl/log trace** (12+ instances, worst in QF-4 where 10 of gpt-5's react windows vanished entirely).
4. **C2 — Library-manipulation effects (scry/Ponder/SDT/Counterbalance) are opaque: revealed cards never shown, sub-decisions never routed** (12 instances, 2 matches; kills entire archetypes' game plans).
5. **A1 — Land/permanent state under-specified: name-deduped counts, no per-copy tapped state** (5 instances; caused one fully unforced misplay — a wasted Rishadan Port activation).
6. **B1 — Combat priority-window gaps: no true post-blocks window, reactive dedup collapses distinct windows** (2 instances; cost one game a turn-8 lethal line).
7. **D3 — latency_ms conflates dead-retry time with real think time; failed first replies are overwritten, not preserved** (8 instances; corrupts any downstream latency/quality analysis).
8. **C4 — Fixed-length truncation (70-char oracle text, 90-char events/plan) drops load-bearing clauses mid-word** (5 instances, mostly non-decisive but a real defect).
9. **F2 — "Fast" tier has no enforced speed/size cap; degenerate generation goes unstopped** (5 instances; one reply degenerated into 157x "CHOICE: 1").
10. **C3 — Bridge-down fallbacks override the model's just-stated plan** (2 solid instances; converts an infra hiccup into a strategic own-goal).
11. **C6 / C7 — Labeling/observability nits**: forced single-legal picks mislabeled `tier=smart` (7 rows); `MULLIGAN_HEURISTIC` auto-keeps with no jsonl record (8 keeps across 6 matches).
12. **A2 — "COUNT LETHAL" hint is life-total-only, misleading for poison/infect decks** (1 confirmed instance, no demonstrated harm yet).

## 4. Per-category detail (A–F)

### Category A — Missing/opaque game-state information

**A1 — Land/permanent state under-specified** (`is_real=true`, confidence high). `landSummary()` (LlmSpikeGui.java:418-429) reports a true numeric total but dedupes land *names* via a `LinkedHashSet`, so the model sees `"7 untapped / 7 total (Volcanic Island, Ancient Tomb, Island)"` — 3 names standing for 7 cards, unreconcilable for color counting. Recurs verbatim at F-1 decision_lines 82, 90, 99. Concrete harm, QF-3 decision_line 66 (kimi-k3, turn 14): raw_reply = *"Port the untapped Volcanic Island... CHOICE: 8"* — but the option list carried three identical, unlabeled `Volcanic Island` entries. Log ground truth (lines 692, 734-735) confirms the chosen copy (id 78) was tapped on turn 13 and never untapped for kimi — the Rishadan Port activation did nothing. No amount of reasoning can disambiguate three identical strings; this is a fully unforced, harness-caused misfire.

**C2 — Library manipulation opaque** (`is_real=true`, confidence high — category A). Every "look at top N" effect (Sensei's Divining Top peek, Ponder's look, Delver's flip check, Preordain's scry) is exposed only as an opaque CAST/Yes-No — revealed card identities are never surfaced, and the reorder/keep-or-bottom sub-decision is never asked. QF-1: Counterbalance's own "may reveal to counter" trigger fired 8x (log lines 622, 712, 760, 769, 779, 826, 889, 895) and never once became an LLM decision, despite gemini repeatedly stating a plan to "set up the top of the library for Counterbalance" — the deck's namesake combo was structurally dead all game. This drove gemini into a pattern of near-identical repeated SDT reasoning that consumed real latency for zero board impact. SF-2 decision_line 25: *"Do you want to shuffle the library?"* — the three looked-at Ponder cards never named — produced the single most vacant reply in the corpus, a bare `CHOICE: 1` with zero reasoning. SF-2: a Preordain scry-2-to-bottom event occurs in the log with **no preceding [ROUTER] line at all** — auto-resolved by the engine — while a Brainstorm put-back the same game *was* routed, showing the gap is inconsistent even within one match.

**A2 — Poison-blind "COUNT LETHAL" hint** (`is_real=true`, confidence medium). LlmSpikeGui.java:582-583 hardcodes: *"COUNT LETHAL: if your unblocked/unstoppable damage meets their life total, attack with everything needed and WIN NOW."* SF-1 decision_line 57: deepseek-v4-pro is playing Infect against an opponent sitting at 20 life who ultimately loses via poison, never dropping below 20 life the entire game (log-confirmed); the harness's own hint points the model at the wrong number. On the cited decision the model's reply was a terse `PICKS: 1` with no visible reasoning artifact of being misled, and deepseek won anyway — so no demonstrated harm yet, but the mechanism is a live landmine for any future infect/poison matchup.

### Category B — Sequencing / priority-window gaps

**B1 — No true post-blocks priority window; reactive dedup over-collapses** (`is_real=true`, confidence medium). `reactSig` (LlmSpikeGui.java:785-788) is computed as `safeStackTop|stackSize|menuStr` — it omits the current phase/combat-step, so a decline at COMBAT_BEGIN and the later declare-blockers-step or pre-damage window (same empty stack, same menu) hash identically and get silently deduped to PASS with no re-ask and no [ROUTER] line. SF-1 decision_line 54: deepseek's own stated plan was *"pump Agent to 5/5 with Might of Old Krosa, then hold Berserk until confirmed-unblocked... for exactly 10 poison"* — a textbook execution of its win condition. But the post-blocks window that should have re-offered Berserk was deduped against the earlier COMBAT_BEGIN pass; by decision_line 58 (combat damage) Berserk is no longer in the menu — too late. Log-confirmed: only 5 poison landed that turn, not 10; the kill slipped two turns. QF-2 decision_line 42 shows the same root cause (a computed pump line at declare-attackers with no reachable post-blocks window to execute it), though the specific "9 infect for lethal" claim in that instance is itself a model miscalculation (instant-speed Might is +2/+2, not the +4/+4 main-phase value) — the sequencing gap is real, the exact lethality math in that one instance is not.

### Category C — Routing / fallback / observability bugs

**C1 — Sacrifice and combat-damage assignment hardcoded to Forge AI** (`is_real=true`, confidence high). `choosePermanentsToSacrifice` (LlmSpikeGui.java:1381-1390) and `assignCombatDamage` (1339-1356) both call `super.*` (Forge's built-in AI) unconditionally, then only *observe* via `observeAiDec` — logging `tier="ai"`, null prompt/raw_reply, no [ROUTER] line, no model consultation at all. QF-3 decision_line 82: `sacrifice(ai) [Emrakul, the Aeons Torn] -> ... Krenko, Mob Boss` — the AI sacrificed kimi-k3's single best remaining threat one turn before it lost. F-1 decision_line 57: a 6-permanent board wipe (Noble Hierarch > Blighted Agent > Noble Hierarch > Forest > Tropical Island > Pendelhaven) chosen entirely by Forge AI. SF-1 decision_line 29: even a *forced* n_options=1 damage-assign case still routes through the AI-delegation path rather than a deterministic `tier=auto` resolution. Note on framing: the jsonl `[TALLY] ai=0` invariant technically still reads 0 in every log (this is a separate, uncounted observation channel that never trips the literal `[FALLBACK->AI]` marker) — so by the strictest letter of the log invariant it is not "violated." But the substantive rule ("never fall back to Forge AI for strategy") is squarely broken, and the pre-computed stats (which count jsonl `tier="ai"`) correctly report `ai>0` for 4 of 8 models.

**C3 — Bridge-down fallbacks override stated intent** (`is_real=true`, confidence high, but scope corrected). F-1 decision_line 43 (deepseek-v4-pro): the model stated *"attack with Blighted Agent alone... Hierarch is unnecessary"* seconds earlier the same turn; two consecutive bridge non-answers triggered `attackers(bridge-down)->all-in x2`, and the log confirms both creatures were declared, negating the exalted single-attacker bonus the model had explicitly planned for. QF-4 decision_line 56 (gpt-5): a stated PLAN named three lands (Tabernacle, Tranquil Thicket, Grove) for Life from the Loam; two bridge non-answers on the targeting decision triggered `target(unparsed-or-dead)->first`, recovering only 1 of 3 lands, silently cutting the spell to a third of its intended value. **Correction to the original cluster**: a third citation claiming this pattern also occurred in QF-2 is unsupported — QF-2's only fallback is a land-play auto-pick, not an attackers-all-in event; the confirmed count is 2 solid instances, not 4.

**C4 — Fixed-length truncation drops load-bearing text** (`is_real=true`, confidence medium). `LlmSpikeGui.java:1538` hard-cuts spell/option descriptions at 70 chars mid-word, no ellipsis; `oneLine()` (~1738-1741) does the same at 90 chars for events and the re-injected turn-plan. QF-2 decision_line 59: Might of Old Krosa's option text literally reads *"...+2/+2 until end of turn. If you cast this spell d"* — cut exactly before the `+4/+4 if it's your main phase` clause. SF-1 decision_line 54: the model's own turn-plan reminder is chopped to *"...attack unblockable, pr"*, dropping the clause naming Berserk as the finisher. **Important caveat verified during review**: in the SF-1 case the true `+4/+4` effect *was* independently visible via the RECENT EVENTS resolution log the same turn, and the model correctly executed its Berserk-holding plan anyway (it was B1's dedup bug, not this truncation, that actually blocked the cast) — so the per-instance behavioral impact is real but smaller than the raw truncation count suggests; on all three cited decisions the model still made the objectively correct play.

**C5 — Silent decision drops on double-failure** (`is_real=true`, confidence high). In `play()`, the bridge-down branch that resolves to PASS (LlmSpikeGui.java:825, 833-838) returns without ever calling `evalDec()` — unlike the `haveLand` branch, which does call `auto()`→`evalDec()`. Result: any double-failure whose legal fallback is PASS (every reactive window, and any main-phase window with no land to play) leaves **zero** jsonl row and **zero** `.log` resolution line. QF-4: gpt-5 has exactly 10 `play(react)` ask windows across turns 4 and 6 (log lines 102, 110, 114, 128, 152, 186, 193, 201, 211, 221); every one hit non-answer twice and vanished — no [AUTO-LEGAL] line, no PASS line, no jsonl entry — while grok-4.5 logs an explicit `play: PASS` every single time it passes in the same match. gpt-5 held a live Punishing Fire (log L157) and Crop Rotation (L165) during exactly these windows and was structurally denied any chance to interact with grok's reanimator combo turns. QF-3: 34 non-answer events in the log produce only 7 jsonl fallback rows — the gap is exactly these silently-PASS-resolved double-failures.

**C6 — Forced single-legal auto-picks mislabeled `tier="smart"`** (`is_real=true`, confidence high). Code: LlmSpikeGui.java:1001 sets `tier = (fb==null ? tierOf(true) : "auto")` — when only one legal candidate exists, `fb` stays null, so the row is tagged `smart` with `latency_ms=0`, `prompt=null`, `raw_reply=null`, indistinguishable in aggregate stats from a genuinely dropped smart call. Verified at QF-2 decision_line 5 (qwen3.7-max), QF-3 decision_line 29 (kimi-k3), QF-4 decision_line 8 (gpt-5) — all `n_options=1`, `tier=smart`, `latency_ms=0`, log confirms `"(only legal)"` auto-resolve, no model call made. This is the entire content of qwen3.7-max's pre-computed `empty_reply: 2` — using that field as a fragility signal for qwen is actively misleading. True count across the corpus is 7 (not the 5 originally claimed), spanning 5 matches.

**C7 — `MULLIGAN_HEURISTIC` auto-keeps with no jsonl record** (`is_real=true`, confidence medium, scope corrected). LlmSpikeGui.java:509-511: `if (MULL_HEURISTIC && lands>=2 && lands<=4) { print(...); return true; }` — a pure print-and-return with no `ask()` and no `evalDec()`, so no jsonl line is ever written for the keep. F-1 log line 39: `[claude-fable-5] [ROUTER] mulligan: heuristic KEEP (3/7 lands)` — verified verbatim, and F-1's jsonl has zero claude-fable-5 mulligan rows while deepseek's mulligan the same game *was* routed to the model. **Correction to the original cluster**: the heuristic is applied symmetrically to both seats (it fires whenever land count is 2-4, regardless of seat) — the routing divergence in F-1/QF-4 is because the *opponent* held 1 land (outside the 2-4 window), not because of any per-seat bias, and the phenomenon is broader than the original 2-match claim: heuristic keeps fired in 8 hands across 6 of the 7 matches. The real, surviving defect is the audit-trail gap (zero model reasoning, zero jsonl record) for a decision that materially matters for combo decks whose keep criterion is not raw land count (Lands/Dark Depths, Sneak-and-Show).

### Category D — Latency / bridge reliability / instrumentation

**D1 — Uniform config produces model-specific instability** (`is_real=true`, confidence medium). `max_tokens:2048` is hardcoded for every model at LlmSpikeGui.java:1845 with no per-model override; no `temperature` or `reasoning_effort` field exists anywhere in the request body; the bridge allows exactly one retry with zero backoff. `isNonAnswer()` (1792-1799) treats empty/rate-limit content as bridge-down — no HTTP 429s appear in any failing log, confirming the failures are the empty-content path (reasoning-token exhaustion under the cap, or timeout), not rate limiting. Decisive natural experiment: QF-2, same match, same bridge — deepseek-v4-pro had 10 non-answer events, qwen3.7-max had 0. SF-1: all 5 non-answers were deepseek, opus had 0. The failing models are exactly the slow, verbose reasoners (deepseek up to 326540ms for a single reply; gpt-5 up to 73029ms). **Correction**: two of the original four supporting citations were found inaccurate on review — the QF-3 "34 non-answer hits for kimi-k3" figure is actually the match *total*; only 23 belong to kimi (the other 11 are claude-fable-5's), which slightly undercuts the framing that claude-fable "fails ~never." The F-1 citation pointed at the wrong decision_line index (a successful call, not the described 326540ms failure sequence — the real event is at zone-change index 37 + auto-legal index 39, log lines 178-187). The underlying claim and QF-2/SF-1 evidence remain solid; the raw jsonl-counted "fallbacks" stat also *understates* the true failure volume, since most non-answer pairs recover on retry and never write a fallback row (see D3, C5) — so if anything the severity framing is conservative, not inflated.

**D3 — latency_ms conflates dead-retry time with think-time; failed replies overwritten** (`is_real=true`, confidence high). Code-confirmed mechanism: LlmSpikeGui.java:1420-1422 sums `lastAskMs` across *all* attempts ("a decision may ask more than once"), and only the *last* attempt's prompt/raw_reply are retained — the first, failed reply is discarded and unrecoverable for diagnosis. SF-1 decision_line 16: jsonl `latency_ms=51918`, but the log shows the successful retry alone `"replied in 7292 ms"` — ~44.6s of dead first-attempt time is silently folded into a field used to judge model "think cost." QF-4 decision_line 55: jsonl `73029`ms vs. log-confirmed real reply time of `19123`ms. QF-2 decision_line 40: jsonl `90129`ms vs. real `34509`ms. SF-1 decision_line 8: two consecutive attempts (37223ms + 13757ms) collapse into one 50981ms row; the first reply's content is gone, so *why* it failed to parse is not auditable after the fact. This is a pure instrumentation defect, independent of any model's actual behavior, and it currently corrupts every cross-model latency comparison in this report's own scorecard (§3) unless read with this caveat in mind.

### Category F — Tier-contract enforcement

**F2 — Fast tier has no enforced cap; degenerate generation goes unstopped** (`is_real=true`, confidence high). The "fast" tier is enforced purely by prompt wording — LlmSpikeGui.java:1845 applies the identical `max_tokens=2048` to both smart and fast calls, no tier-specific timeout, no `reasoning_effort`, no stop-sequence, no repetition detector. Result: kimi-k3 fast-tier median is 57873ms, worse than most models' *smart*-tier latency; SF-1 shows deepseek's fast-tier median (10497ms) exceeding opus's smart-tier median (3061ms) in the same match — the fast tier delivers none of its intended speed benefit for slow-reasoning backends. QF-3 decision_line 21: a fast-tier call from kimi-k3 produced a full paragraph of reasoning (561 chars) taking 71166ms before ending `"CHOICE: 2"` — the model simply ignored the terse-reply instruction, and the harness has no mechanism to enforce it. QF-1 decision_line 87: a gemini-3.5-flash fast-tier reply degenerated into exactly 157 repetitions of `"CHOICE: 1"` (2390 chars total — a genuine defect, though this is only a fraction of the 2048-token cap, not "most of it" as originally overstated) — the reply still parsed correctly (valid tag present), but produced the match's largest single latency (11241ms) for no reasoning benefit.

## 5. Prioritized proposals

### Prompt additions

1. **Untapped-mana-by-color line in snapshot** *(evidence: A1)* — Rendered into all 575 decision prompts. Replace the name-deduped land line with a per-color untapped tally computed by walking each battlefield land's mana abilities: `mana available: {W:0 U:2 B:1 R:1 G:0 C:1} (3 untapped sources)`, for both self and opponent (the latter is explicitly called load-bearing by the code's own comment at LlmSpikeGui.java:384-386). **Location**: `landSummary()`, 418-429, called at 379/386. **Payoff**: kills the entire class of "can I cast this / can they counter" misreads. **Confidence: likely.**

2. **Per-copy land listing + [tapped]/[untapped] flag on every target candidate** *(evidence: A1)* — One entry per land copy (or an `xN` split by tap state) so untapped/total reconciles; append a `[tapped]`/`[untapped]` suffix to duplicate-named permanents in every target/entity option list (`perms()` at 469-493 already computes `c.isTapped()` — reuse it in the label builders at 857-887, 921-962, 969-1006). **Payoff**: removes the one fully unforced misplay class found in the corpus — no amount of model reasoning can disambiguate three identical strings. **Confidence: strong.**

3. **Poison-aware COUNT LETHAL block** *(evidence: A2, B1)* — Replace the hardcoded life-only hint at LlmSpikeGui.java:582-583 with a pre-computed, archetype-aware line: detect infect/poison sources among legal attackers, compute both `opp life` and `10 - opp.poison`, and always show `poison X/10` even at 0 (currently hidden by the `po==0` branch at line 464). **Payoff**: stops the harness from actively pointing an infect deck at the wrong target number. **Confidence: likely.**

4. **Surface revealed library-manipulation cards + full stack contents/priority holder** *(evidence: C2)* — Fold revealed card identities from scry/Ponder/SDT into a `REVEALED (top of library): ...` line near the stack block (390-391), reusing the existing INTEL-deque `remember()` pattern (216-223, 1033, 1171); expand `safeStackTop` (411-417) to enumerate the full stack with controllers/targets and an explicit `PRIORITY: you` marker. **Payoff**: turns every library-manipulation and reactive decision from a blind guess into an informed one — a prerequisite for the C2 mechanics fix below to matter. **Confidence: likely.**

5. **Word-boundary truncation with ellipsis, raised caps for effect-bearing text** *(evidence: C4)* — Cut at the last whitespace before the limit and append `"..."` instead of a raw mid-word `substring(0,70)`/`substring(0,90)` (LlmSpikeGui.java:1538, ~1738-1741); raise the option-text cap for numeric/conditional clauses; never truncate the re-injected turn-plan. **Payoff**: low-risk string fix that stops severing conditional pump values and the model's own stated finisher clause. **Confidence: likely.**

### Mechanics

1. **Route sacrifice and combat-damage assignment through the LLM pipeline** *(evidence: C1)* — Rewrite `choosePermanentsToSacrifice` (1381-1390) and `assignCombatDamage` (1339-1356) to build a numbered candidate menu and route through `decideMany`/`decideOne`, mirroring the existing failure ladder in `chooseCardsToDiscardFrom` (1008-1040): unparsed/bridge-down → deterministic first-N, genuinely forced n=1 → `tier="auto"`, never Forge AI. ### Infra

1. **Per-model config table (max_tokens, timeout, reasoning_effort, temperature)** *(evidence: D1, F2)* — A static `Map<modelId, ModelCfg>` consulted at the OpenRouter body builder (1838-1845) and the Claude CLI argv (1663-1664). Evidence-grounded starting values: deepseek-v4-pro and gpt-5 → 16000 tokens + medium reasoning-effort (both hit the cap hard under current config); kimi-k3 → 16000 tokens, 150000ms timeout (its own median latencies already exceed the 90s default); grok-4.5 → 8000 tokens; terse models (opus, gemini, qwen, claude-fable) keep ~4000/90s. **Payoff**: directly removes the fairness-distorting config interaction the QF-2 natural experiment isolates. **Confidence: strong.**

2. **Per-tier output caps + stop-on-tag + repetition guard** *(evidence: F2)* — Give the fast tier a materially lower `max_tokens` (~256) and shorter timeout (~20-30s) in the same config table; add a stop sequence on the first valid CHOICE/PICKS line, and a cheap n-gram repetition detector in `OpenRouterBridge.ask` (1835-1875). **Payoff**: gives "fast" the latency guarantee its name promises; prevents a single degenerate reply from burning the whole budget. **Confidence: likely.**

3. **`finish_reason` truncation detection + retry-at-larger-budget** *(evidence: D1, C4)* — `extractJsonString` (1763-1786) never reads `finish_reason`; add a scan for `"finish_reason":"length"`, and on a length-truncated + unparsed reply, retry once at a larger `max_tokens` instead of an identical re-ask, logging a distinct `truncated->retry` reason. **Payoff**: distinguishes "ran out of tokens" from "bridge died," recovering verbose reasoners currently indistinguishable from dead connections. **Confidence: likely.**

4. **Robust CHOICE/PICKS extraction tolerant of markdown/separators** *(evidence: D1, F2)* — Harden `parseTagNumber` (1549-1554) to strip markdown fences/bold before matching and accept separators beyond `:` (`CHOICE - 3`, `Answer: 3`), while preserving the deliberate LAST-occurrence semantics (1541-1548). 5. **Multi-retry with backoff + per-seat health circuit-breaker** *(evidence: D1, C5)* — Replace the single flat re-ask in `ask()` (1402-1424) with ~3 attempts and exponential backoff; track consecutive failures per seat/tier and trip a loud, logged circuit-breaker (downgrade to the existing deterministic policy) instead of silently auto-playing for 40+ minutes, as happened to gpt-5 in QF-4. **Payoff**: converts a silent multi-turn degradation into an observable, bounded one. **Confidence: likely.**

6. **Observability overhaul: guaranteed jsonl row, split latency, retry-count, `finish_reason` field** *(evidence: C5, D3, C6)* — Make the `play()` bridge-down→PASS branch (825, 833-838) always call `evalDec()`; record successful-attempt latency separately from dead-retry latency at 1420-1422 and add `bridge_retries`/`first_failed_reply` fields; tag forced single-legal picks `tier="auto"` at 1001. **Payoff**: makes the eval corpus trustworthy — no more invisible dropped decisions, no laundered think-time, no conflation of "no call needed" with "call failed." A precondition for using these logs to rank models at all. **Confidence: strong.**

- Tag forced single-legal auto-picks `tier="auto"` with a reason at line 1001 (C6) — unpollutes per-tier stats immediately.

**Bigger builds** (structural):
- Per-model/provider-adaptive bridge layer (`ModelCfg` table + `finish_reason`-aware retry/backoff + health circuit-breaker) — the single highest-leverage build; de-confounds every future eval (D1/F2/C5).

- Route every currently observe-only Forge-AI surface (sacrifice, combat-damage, order-blockers/attackers, announce-requirements, modal-repeat abilities, multi-target `chooseTargetsFor`) through the LLM pipeline — establishes the `ai=0` invariant in substance, not just in the log counter (C1 + documented known-gaps).
- Hidden-zone / revealed-information model: persist Thoughtseize-style reveals, scry peeks, and exile contents into a structured snapshot field instead of the ad-hoc 8-entry INTEL deque (C2 + known-gap omits).
- Migrate off regex/hand-rolled-JSON parsing to a structured-output/tool-use decision API, eliminating the whole `no_choice_tag`/truncation/PLAN-comingling parse-failure class at its root.
- Trustworthy-eval observability layer: guaranteed one jsonl row per decision (including double-failures and reactive PASSes), split think-time/dead-retry latency, `bridge_retries`/`finish_reason` fields, reconciled against the far-larger `.log` non-answer volume this analysis found (C5/D3/C6).

## 7. What was NOT the harness's fault

Three misplays in the corpus were verified to be genuine model reasoning errors with adequate information and budget present in the prompt — these must not be reattributed to infra:

1. **grok-4.5, SF-2 decision_line 30** (decisive, high impact): given a free Thoughtseize choice among Sneak Attack / Show and Tell / Force of Will in the opponent's fully revealed hand, grok stripped Force of Will — a reactive counter it had no way to interact with, never holding mana up on the opponent's turn — instead of either combo-enabler. Fable resolved the un-stripped Sneak Attack next turn and closed the game two turns later. Full information was present; this was strategic judgment, not an info gap.
2. **claude-opus-4.8, SF-1 decision_line 74** (high impact): the prompt correctly stated the opponent had 1 untapped land, yet opus reasoned "they can't pay {1} for Daze" — a plain arithmetic error, since 1 untapped land pays a generic {1}. deepseek paid the tax, resolved Invigorate, and dealt the winning 5 poison.
3. **claude-opus-4.8, QF-1 decision_line 105** (benign): a Brainstorm put-back reply that visibly contradicts itself mid-thought before settling on a reasonable final pick — a reasoning-quality wobble with no bad outcome, flagged for honesty only.

Three further instances are **mixed** — a real model error, but with a harness defect materially contributing (kimi-k3's Krenko lethal miscount, partly caused by X-value truncation in option text; deepseek's hallucinated Exalted trigger on a card with no such ability, with no oracle text available to check against; deepseek's mana-illegal Invigorate plan, made easier to state inconsistently by the isolated per-window call structure and truncated PLAN text). These are documented in §4 under their respective clusters and are not counted as clean harness defects.

**Refuted clusters** (submitted for review, found not to hold up): *D2* claimed `CLAUDE_DEADLINE_MS` (90s) goes unenforced for replies far past deadline — refuted because every cited long-latency seat used the OpenRouter backend, governed by the separate `OPENROUTER_TIMEOUT_MS` variable, not `CLAUDE_DEADLINE_MS` at all; the long accepted latencies reflect a generously configured timeout, not a broken one, and the cited 326540ms reply was in fact the objectively correct choice — enforcing a hard cutoff as proposed would have replaced a valid answer with a worse one. *F1* claimed forced single-option decisions were wastefully routed to full smart-tier calls — refuted because the code already short-circuits genuinely forced singles (LlmSpikeGui.java:869, 898) before any model call; the cited "forced" decisions actually carried a real decline option or genuine strategic content (e.g., whether to attack with 0 vs. 1 creature), and auto-resolving them as proposed would have caused real misplays. *B2* claimed multi-target effects were wrongly split into sequential single-select calls — refuted because the harness's multi-target override structurally delegates such spells to Forge AI targeting rather than sequential LLM calls (925-928), and the cited "sequential" evidence was either defensible engine behavior (Brainstorm's two-card put-back, which puts cards on top in order) or a misread auto-fallback, not a model CHOICE at all.
