# IRT on existing puzzle results: a pilot of the method, not a calibration (2026-11-05)

Free, read-only. The fitting scripts (a stdlib Rasch and 2PL fit by joint and conditional maximum likelihood, with
attempt-level bootstrap intervals) were not kept in the repository; the numbers below are their output. Pooling followed `puzzle_score.py`'s rules (one suite sha, one epoch, one condition per solver).

## Bottom line
No results matrix on disk can calibrate puzzle difficulty for ranking models yet.
- The only many-model matrix (frontier5) has 5 puzzles, one game per cell, and mixed conditions.
- The only matrix with repeats (rules suite) has one model (Luna, 4 configurations) plus zero-model arms.
- The zero-model arms do not behave like IRT respondents.

## Rules suite (29 puzzles, 9 respondents, 2,436 attempts)
- floor and pass solve nothing and are dropped, leaving 7 fitted respondents.
- 18 of 29 puzzles are solved on every Luna attempt. Only 11 separate the Luna arms.

| Respondent | pass@1 | Rasch ability (SE) | infit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luna v1-on / v1-off | 0.897 | 2.91 (0.34) | 0.80 / 2.08 |
| Luna e67-on | 0.876 | 2.60 (0.31) | 0.81 |
| Luna e67-off | 0.821 | 1.95 (0.26) | 1.63 |
| Forge AI | 0.302 | -1.20 (0.22) | 2.99 |
| first | 0.207 | -1.78 (0.24) | 3.67 |
| random | 0.088 | -2.88 (0.10) | 1.69 |

- **Zero-model arms misfit.** first and Forge AI are near-deterministic (infit 3.7 and 3.0). Random's difficulty order
  matches Luna's at only Kendall tau-b 0.12. They should stay floors, not calibrate difficulty.
- **2PL:** discrimination ranges 0.27 to 2.81, none negative. It is not identifiable here: the respondents form two
  clusters, Luna and the floors.
- **Puzzles to audit:**
  - RP_477 (infit 19.5): Luna solves it only with tools on, random 9 of 50.
  - RP_443 (outfit 7.2).
- **A smaller set:** the top-5 most informative puzzles agree with the full ranking at 0.62, against a median of 0.84
  for a random 5-puzzle subset. Adaptive selection did not beat a random subset.

## frontier5 (20 models x 5 boards, k = 1, mixed caps, deadlines and token limits; exploratory)
- 12 of 20 models are informative: 5 solve all boards and 3 solve none.
- Difficulty, conditional ML (SE about 0.8): atog -1.49, drain2 -0.97, splice2 -0.11, hoofdrain 0.69, elf2 1.88.
- Each model's ability SE (1.0 to 1.5) is wider than one board's step. This is FRONTIER5's "one or two solves is not a
  ranking" in IRT form.
- 2PL is not identifiable. The top-k selection performs like the median random subset.

## What a real calibration needs
- Several models on one suite, one epoch and one condition.
- At least 4 attempts per cell, so attempts can be split.
- Puzzles that are neither saturated for the models nor at zero for everyone.

IRT then adds intervals on ability and difficulty, but on a complete matrix it gives the same order as pass@1.

## Not determined
- Model-level discrimination, and the negative-discrimination audit BENCHMARK-V2 plans.
- Parameter stability under resampling respondents. Every interval resamples attempts only, so all are too narrow.
- Whether adaptive selection helps on a suite many models answer.
